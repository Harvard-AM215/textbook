Section 6 — Compartmental flows and epidemic modeling

Optional practice resource; no submission. Section time after the quiz is for project work.

Use this simple outbreak model to practice reading populations and flows. The numbers are illustrative, not fitted COVID-19 parameters. Assume a closed population, homogeneous mixing, and no reinfection during the modeled period. S,I,RS,I,RS,I,R count susceptible, infectious, and recovered people, with S+I+R=NS+I+R=NS+I+R=N.

S˙=−βSI,I˙=βSI−γI,R˙=γI.\dot S=-\beta SI,\qquad \dot I=\beta SI-\gamma I,\qquad \dot R=\gamma I.S˙=−βSI,I˙=βSI−γI,R˙=γI.
(1)

A. Populations and flows¶

  1. Draw the arrows between compartments and label each with its flow rate. Explain why the same infection term appears with opposite signs. Give the units of β\betaβ and γ\gammaγ when time is in days.

  2. Take N=10,000N=10{,}000N=10,000, S=8,000S=8{,}000S=8,000, I=100I=100I=100, R=1,900R=1{,}900R=1,900, β=2×10−5\beta=2\times10^{-5}β=2×10−5 per person per day, and γ=0.1\gamma=0.1γ=0.1 per day. Find the infection and recovery flows and all three derivatives. Check conservation. Is III the number of new infections each day?

B. Contact changes and early growth¶

  1. At this same state, contact changes halve β\betaβ. Recalculate I˙\dot II˙. Which flows change immediately? Which compartment sizes change instantaneously, and why does a negative I˙\dot II˙ not mean there are no infectious people?

  2. Early in an outbreak, S≈NS\approx NS≈N. Derive the exponential growth rate rrr of III. If data give r=0.10r=0.10r=0.10 per day, can you determine both β\betaβ and γ\gammaγ? Now use an independent estimate γ=0.10\gamma=0.10γ=0.10 per day to find β\betaβ and R0=βN/γR_0=\beta N/\gammaR0​=βN/γ.

C. Extensions for COVID modeling¶

  1. Add an exposed compartment EEE: infected people who are not yet infectious. Replace S→IS\to IS→I by S→E→IS\to E\to IS→E→I, with progression flow κE\kappa EκE and transmission caused by III. Write the four equations and check conservation. If new exposures stop, why can people still become infectious afterward?

  2. Add a return flow from RRR to SSS at rate ωR\omega RωR to represent loss of immunity. Which equations change? Suggest one other compartment or observation process a COVID model might need, and one kind of data needed to estimate its new parameter. Distinguish daily reported cases from the model’s infectious population.
